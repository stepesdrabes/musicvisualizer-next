What the analyser gets wrong, measured

A full audit of the DSP-to-light path against annotated ground truth, plus a costed answer to whether an on-device model earns its place. Every number here was produced by running the code, not read from a paper.

A dark room, and the two bugs behind it

Reported from the app: a track loaded, the timeline and the arrangement drew, and the room stayed black with "Track ready" on screen. Two separate faults, one of them mine and one of them a failure mode that has been waiting there all along.

The planner sized a hit at one bar and placed it at another. A strobe's length is capped in seconds; the cap was evaluated with barDurationAt at the bar the strobe was measured from, and the hit was then written a bar earlier. On a grid that drifts by a per cent - which is every real track, and 16% of the corpus by more - the two disagree, so the engine wrote a strobe of 3.02 s against its own 3.0 s cap. Both sides now call one function, hitSeconds in grid.ts, which reads the span off the bar table. The same class of defect as the two clocks the first pass found, and the same fix: one place owns the arithmetic.

And the app discards a show the linter rejects, without saying so. if (!verdict.ok) show = null in the ingest route, which leaves the room dark and the panel reading "No show yet" - no error, no finding, nothing to act on. A show with one over-long strobe in it is strictly better than no show at all, and a rejection the user cannot see is worse than either. This has not been changed here, because what an invalid show should do is a product decision rather than a bug fix, but it is the reason a small planner defect presented as a completely dead application.

The guard. composeShow now has to survive its own linter across seven tempos from 58 to 175 bpm, on flat grids and on grids jittered a few per cent per bar, twelve seeds each. The jitter matters and a smooth ramp does not: the fault only appears where neighbouring bars fall on OPPOSITE sides of a cap, so the first two versions of this test passed against the broken code. It was only worth keeping once it failed against it.

The metric had to be built before anything could be said

Neither mean brightness nor frame-to-frame change can see a blink: a slow swell moves every pixel every frame and looks calm, while a shimmer of a few bytes is invisible in an average. The second difference is no better, because it cannot tell jitter from the curvature of a swell - the same mistake shape made in the pass before. What works is a high-pass: subtract each pixel's own quarter-second average and take what is left. Ripple is that residual, in bytes. It is blind to a swell of any speed or shape and sees nothing else.

Three routes, all of them continuous, all of them into brightness

  • The spectrum drove brightness. Each band is normalised across the whole track, so a quiet intro's bass sits at byte 166 of 255 - the encoding says loud where the music is quiet - and every band carries 5-8 bytes of jitter per frame at 50 fps. Effects now spend the spectrum on COLOUR and POSITION only. Brightness comes from the section, the cue and the bar table.
  • Nothing was latched to the grid. BeatHold in the DSL reads its input once per beat and holds it, with a glide measured in beats: long for a position so it arrives, short for a colour so it steps. Every change the room makes now lands on a beat by construction.
  • f.energy is beat-resolution data that the player interpolates per frame, so a brightness multiplied by it slides continuously - the same shimmer by another route, and the one that survived the first two fixes. Latched in the six effects that can appear in a quiet cue.

The largest single cause was an idiom used in the wrong place. fadeToBlack plus addSample keeps a fraction of the last frame and adds the full pattern on top, which is exactly right for trails and sparks. For a field that MOVES it leaves a lagging edge on every pixel the pattern crosses, and that edge ripples at the frame rate however slowly the pattern itself travels. Measured alone on a real intro, bandBloom rippled at 4.23 bytes and harmonicRibbon at 3.91, against 0.72 for the worst bed in the catalog. Both are steady fields and both now write every pixel every frame; that one change took the intro from 4.09 to 1.12.

Two smaller things went with it: a breakdown drew its accent from the same pool as a drop, so a passage with the drums taken out could be lit by stage blinders (ripple 2.32), and the quiet sections' motion was still 0.55-0.7 where the instruction was "nearly still". They are 0.24-0.34 now.

Every effect was being handed the wrong colour

The palette is a ring of anchors that effects sample by named slot, and the slots sat at 0.06, 0.22, 0.38, 0.5, 0.64, 0.8 and 0.94 on a ring of 16. Only one of those is a whole anchor. SLOT.accent fell at position 12.8, so asking for the accent returned four fifths of an anchor already fading toward accentDeep blended with one fifth of an anchor sitting 69% of the way from the third hue toward it: up to 0.19 of RGB distance from the colour the show declared, on the one slot a drop is lit with.

Fifty anchors, and every slot lands exactly on one. Measured against hsv2rgb of the declared hue directly, the error is now 0.000 at every slot for every palette tested, and there is a test asserting it. The share of lit bytes on a hue the palette could produce went from 97.4% to 99.4% across the cache.

What this was NOT. The first measurement said the declared accent hue appeared nowhere on the ring, out by up to 37 degrees. That is hsv2rgb doing its documented job: it is FastLED's rainbow ramp, not textbook HSV, with a widened yellow band because textbook HSV reads wrong on LEDs. Comparing the ring against the ramp's own output rather than against a hue angle is what separated the real defect from the deliberate one, and the ramp was left alone.

A bar of black is four beats

The fourth pass fixed a held breath that ended mid-bar by making it a whole bar, which at the tempos this repertoire actually sits at is three seconds of nothing. That is the same mistake at the other end.

Punctuation is now counted in whole BEATS rather than whole bars, and the rule is that a hit touches a downbeat at one end or the other. A gesture whose whole job is the thing that follows it may start inside its bar, which is the only way to be short and still land exactly on the drop: at 77 bpm the held breath is now two beats and 1.6 s, at 128 bpm four beats and 1.9 s. Longest blackout in the cache 3.1 s to 2.1 s, median 0.75 bars. All 200 planned hits still fire and every show still lints clean.

Only part of the room lit up

A photograph of an intro showed a bright ceiling beam over near-black walls. The cue was a bed and one accent, and both were measured afterwards: the accent lit 28% of the room with nearly half its output in the brightest tenth of the pixels, and it declared carries: true. So did six others that could not.

carries is what the planner trusts when it picks the single texture over a bed in a quiet cue, so a false claim there is not a documentation slip, it is a dark room. Seven were corrected from measurement, and a test now holds the metadata to it: a bed claiming to carry must light at least 22% of the room, keep under a third of its light in the brightest tenth, and leave its dimmest wall no worse than 35% of its brightest.

Five effects were added to refill the pool, all of them driven by the spectrum or the beat grid rather than by drums, because the passages that were dark are the ones with no drums in them: harmonicHaze (a noise field whose grain follows how busy the mix is, 88% of the room), phraseArc (a swell that completes on the phrase, 79%), bandBloom (low, middle and top of the mix as three overlapping fields, 95%), breathe (a two-bar cycle for passages that should feel held), and spectrumRings. spectrumBed was fixed: it lit 4% of the room, because its quiet end sat below the authoring value of 0.335 that gamma 2.2 puts at byte 24.

One effect was deleted rather than repaired. tideline was written as a waterline rising up the walls, and the room has no vertical extent: every strip sits at the wall/ceiling junction, so the coordinate it was reading is floor-plan depth, not height. It lit the south wall and left the north wall at byte 0.1 at every tide. The premise was wrong for this room, so it went.

And one metric was wrong, which is worth recording because it flattered the fourth pass. `shape`, the fourth pass's measure of "the room has structure", is mean absolute deviation across the frame, and a bright beam over dark walls scores high on it. It cannot tell structure from patchiness. The measure that answers the actual complaint is the dimmest strip against the brightest, which is 1.88x on average across the quiet sections of 24 tracks and 5.2x on the worst of them, down from 7.5x. That worst case is still open.

The arrangement now comes from the drums

arrange() received kicksPerBar and snaresPerBar and spent them on three event tags while every label was decided by an energy quantile. Four fifths of every track came out groove, so the show had one instruction for most of its runtime.

The kit turns out to separate the vocabulary cleanly, and the separation was measured before anything was built. Per beat, across 374 tracks: a drop carries 1.69 kicks and 1.41 snares, a groove 1.44 and 1.00, a build 1.06 and 0.63, and half a breakdown's bars carry no kit at all against all of a groove's. Three rules follow directly, and each was measured on its own:

  • A breakdown is where the kit stops, not where the level drops. The one test in the labeller that goes nowhere near loudness, which is the point: a filtered breakdown sits at the same loudness as the groove around it. Worth 1.5 points of groove share and +0.1 of pairwise F: free.
  • A build is read backwards from the drop it points at, and now walks back through more than one segment while the trajectory holds. A segmenter that split a sixteen-bar riser in two left only its second half touching the drop, and the rest was called groove. Free on every metric.
  • A drop is where the kit returns with the bottom. Yadati et al. (ISMIR 2014) define the drop as the moment the buildup ends and the bassline is re-introduced, and add rhythm features to a spectral classifier precisely because the rhythm switches there; both halves are required here. This is the expensive one: 11 points of groove share for 0.8 of pairwise F.

A fourth rule came out of the research question about SectionSpan.group: a repeat of a drop is a drop, guarded by the kit so a stripped reprise is not promoted. Worth 7.6 points of groove share, +0.2 of F0.5 and +0.2 of F3 for 0.3 of pairwise.

Repeat identity should not decide labels on its own, and that was settled by measurement rather than by argument. Scoring SectionSpan.group directly as the pairwise partition over 374 tracks gives 49.2% against the seven-value lighting vocabulary's 53.8%. The grouper is less reliable than the labeller, so identity is used here as a tie-breaker on drum evidence and never as a substitute for it.

The pairwise cost is real and it is a trade, not a defect. Per track: 103 score worse, 206 unchanged, 65 better, with comparable magnitudes at both ends. The dial is DROP_KICK_STEP in arrange.ts; at 9 the drum route is off and the numbers are groove 66.1% with pairwise 53.6%. It sits at 0.3. Worth remembering that SALAMI is folk, jazz and classical as well as dance, and this rule is written for a repertoire where the kit returning IS the chorus.

All the figures above are from the frozen-cache sweep, which is the instrument the 13.5 / 31.0 / 53.8 targets were themselves measured with. The full 7m36s run from audio confirms them independently at 13.6 / 31.3 / 53.3: the two paths differ slightly because the full run re-derives the drum detection and the void carving that the sweep freezes, and the pairwise cost comes out smaller by that route, not larger.

Effects that react to what is playing

The complaint, verbatim: "when there are no drums/kicks in intro, the effects are just plain color with a little drift, nothing fancy." Accurate, and unfixable with what the contract carried - four band numbers at beat resolution cannot describe a chord, a filter opening or a melody.

TrackAnalysis.spectrum is new: 20 log-spaced bands from 35 Hz to 13.7 kHz at 50 fps, each normalised against its own distribution across the track exactly as the four contract bands are. Stored as one byte per value in a single base64 string, because a four-minute track is a quarter of a million values and pretty-printed JSON numbers would be a megabyte and a quarter of a million lines; as shipped it is 178 kB for a 137-second track. ANALYSIS_VERSION 6 to 7.

What that buys is measurable without watching anything. bench/levelprobe.ts gained two columns: move, how many bytes the average pixel changes frame to frame, and shape, how far the pixels differ from each other within one frame. "Plain colour with a little drift" is a passage with a respectable mean byte and nothing in either column.

Three times the spatial structure in the passages that had none, and contrast improved rather than being spent to buy it. Two new effects (spectrumBed lays the room out low-to-high; harmonicRibbon follows the spectral centroid and tightens as the mix thins to one voice), three reworked (vuTowers gives every strip its own column instead of one drive and a hashed bias, chorusBloom and ambientDrift gained a breath of spectrum), and five DSL helpers that are also vocabulary for generated effects: bandAt, bandBetween, spectrumPeak, spectralTilt, spectrumFocus.

The loose end the third pass left is closed properly. A quiet cue's accent is now required to carry, which was tried before and reverted because exactly one accent in the catalog qualified. There are now two for an intro and two for an outro, so the rule can be what it should always have been: the second layer of a thin cue is a layer.

And the house floor came down with it. SECTION_FLOOR for the quiet three was cut by about a third, because it existed to compensate for cues whose second layer emitted nothing. A compensation left in place after the thing it compensated for is fixed is contrast spent for no reason - the room reads the difference between a verse and a drop, not the absolute level of either. Every quiet section still sits above byte 32 at 99-100% coverage.

Punctuation that lands where the music does

Strobes too long, some starting early, and a blackout that ended before the phrase change it existed to set up. All three were in planHits, and the last one was written down: the held-breath blackout ran max(1, round(beatsPerBar / 2)) beats from the bar before a drop, so the room came back mid-bar, before the downbeat.

The rules are now in the contract and enforced by the linter, so they cannot regress:

  • Every hit starts on a downbeat and lasts a whole number of bars, so it also ends on one. 43 of 91 planned hits were a fractional number of bars long; now none are.
  • Every hit is anchored to something audible: the 4-bar grid, a boundary the analyser measured, a crash in the audio, or the gesture it hands over to. That last clause is what makes strobe → black → slam one figure in three parts rather than three loose hits, and without it the run into a drop is unplaceable, because only its final part lands on a downbeat anyone is counting.
  • The pre-drop blackout runs to the drop downbeat. A whole bar, ending exactly there. 12 of 14 ended early; now none do.
  • A strobe is capped in bars and in seconds. Two bars and 3.0 s, because a bar is 1.4 s at 175 bpm and 3 s at 80 and the complaint was about how long it felt. The longest strobe in the cache was 8.4 s and is now 3.0. Where one bar already exceeds the cap, no strobe is placed: a tempo that cannot fit a bar of it inside three seconds should not be strobing.

Six new linter rules carry this: hit-off-downbeat, hit-part-bar, hit-too-long, hit-too-long-in-seconds, unanchored-hit, blackout-ends-early. Every engine-composed show in the cache lints with zero errors and all 100 planned hits still fire.

One documented decision was deliberately reversed, and it is worth saying which. A test asserted the linter does not limit strobe length, alongside flash rate, budget and minimum gap. Length is now capped and the other three are not, because they are different questions: the rate is the safety dial and belongs to FlashLimiter and WCAG 2.3.1, while the length is taste - a strobe that outlasts the phrase it points at has stopped being punctuation whatever rate it runs at. Nothing in FlashLimiter was touched.

A side effect worth recording: shorter strobes mean the flash limiter engages on 5.7% of frames rather than 7.2%. The limiter was quietly attenuating the back half of every long strobe, so the extra seconds were buying fatigue rather than brightness.

Nothing here moved the grid, and that was checked rather than assumed. The full 764-track tempo, beat, downbeat and meter run comes back exactly flat: all thirteen metrics at +0.0pp against the baseline, tempo Acc1 74.7%, Acc2 94.9%, beat F 78.1%, downbeat F 49.8%, meter 89.9%. End to end on the shipped cache: 100 of 100 planned hits fire, every engine-composed show lints with zero errors, no built-in is left unplaced, 97.4% of lit bytes sit on a hue the frame's palette could produce, and 341 tests pass.

Three things were tried, measured and reverted, on top of the three the last pass recorded. Extending intro and outro through the leading and trailing run of quiet segments took groove share down another 2.7 points and cost 0.5 of pairwise, and the giveaway was in the drum profile: the extended intros came out at 1.07 kicks per beat against a real intro's 0.78, which is groove density wearing an intro's label. A minimum length for a drum-found drop was built on the theory that promoting a short segment fragments a passage the annotation hears as continuous; swept from 0 to 12 bars it moved pairwise by less than 0.2 in either direction, so the theory was wrong and the constant came out. Raising LAMBDA from 1.1 to 2.2, which the brief offered as a way to bring the section count down toward the annotated 11.7, cost 1.5 points of F0.5 and lowered pairwise as well, so fewer segments is not what the pairwise loss wanted.

1  Tempo changes

The grid is fitted as a single constant tempo. When a track drifts, the analyser stores the real beat times in bars[].t but barTimeAt() in packages/core/src/grid.ts recomputes bar times from one averaged period. The player uses barTimeAt for every cue and every hit, so the show runs against a metronome the song is not playing to.

criticalfixedBar times have two sources of truthcore/src/grid.ts:10

Max error 4.848 s (8.5 beats) at bar 111 on Hallowed Be Thy Name, mean 2.678 s over 188 bars. Also 0.862 s on Melanz, 0.644 s on Run To The Hills, 0.290 s on Way Too Self Aware. Four of fifteen cached tracks drift. Fixed: max error is now 0.000000 s.

criticalfixedThe quantiser rewrites drum onsets onto a uniform gridanalysis/src/quantise.ts:37

A single period is averaged over the whole track and slots are laid from beat zero, so on a drifting track the emitted onsets carry the grid's phase rather than the music's. Every shipped kick lies exactly on that uniform line, max residual 0.001 s, which confirms the output is grid times and not audio times. Fixed: slot times interpolate inside real beats; detected hits keep their own time.

highfixedShowFrame carries two disagreeing clockscore/src/player.ts:125

Cues, barIndex, section and energy come off the constant grid; buildProgress, timeToDrop and sectionProgress come off the real section times. At the drop cue on Hallowed Be Thy Name the frame simultaneously reports section drop and timeToDrop 2.26 s. Fixed: every index comes off the bar table.

highfixedtempo.constant is a dead flagauthor-engine/src/lint.ts:39

Nothing reads it. The linter works purely in bar indices and never converts a bar to a time, so it cannot see a timing error by construction. All four drifting shows lint clean with zero errors. Fixed: now descriptive only; barTimes is the authority either way.

highThe drift test measures dispersion, not accumulated phaseanalysis/src/beats.ts:254

A slow monotonic ramp has small dispersion and large accumulated error. 800 beats ramping 100 to 102.4 bpm reports 1.90% spread and is called constant, while the best-fit constant grid is 2.37 beats out. tempoSpread also returns 0 unconditionally for sequences of 64 beats or fewer.

highfixedPhrase anchor is fitted mod 4 and consumed mod 8core/src/player.ts:243

Of 115 section starts across the cached analyses, 112 sit on the 4-bar grid the anchor was fitted to but only 54 (47%) actually fire phraseStart. On Run To The Hills, none of the 7 do. Fixed: the anchor is now searched over a whole phrase and scored on the 4-bar grid first, so the mod-8 phase is decided by evidence rather than by whichever half the search reached first. Over 5,704 section starts on 374 tracks the 4-bar coverage is unchanged at 80% and phraseStart coverage goes from 41% to 51%.

2  Sections

The segmenter is not the problem. The labeller throws its work away.

criticalfixedAdjacent sections sharing a label are merged unconditionallyanalysis/src/arrange.ts:344

Labels come from a four-value vocabulary assigned by energy quantile, so most neighbours match. The merge tests string equality and ignores groups.group entirely. On Jedna Dva, 23 segments become 7 and 14 repeat relationships become 2. A verse-chorus-verse-chorus cannot survive it. Fixed: identity separated from label; merge bounded at 32 bars.

criticalfixedThe DP objective is maximised by chopping everything into 8-bar piecesanalysis/src/structure.ts:169

segmentScore divides by segment length while BAND caps each bar's contribution, so the quotient saturates and adding a boundary is almost free. On a synthetic control with perfect 32-bar blocks it returns 20 segments of 8 bars, not 5 of 32: 6 of 6 true boundaries found, and 15 spurious ones alongside them. Fixed: baseline subtraction, no length normalisation.

highfixedThe length prior is inert at any valueanalysis/src/structure.ts:152

Because the optimum is already all-8-bar and lengthPenalty(8) is zero, sweeping LAMBDA across a 400x range leaves the output bit-identical. MAX_SEGMENT_BARS = 32 is likewise unreachable: the longest segment ever produced across all cached tracks is 10 bars. Fixed: LAMBDA now bites and MAX_SEGMENT_BARS binds.

highfixedrepeatOf indices are never remapped after splicinganalysis/src/arrange.ts:136

Sections are folded, merged and spliced, shifting every index, but repeatOf keeps its old value. On Bulletproof, four surviving values that meant "the drop at bars 20-28" now point at a breakdown. Fixed: derived from a stable group id.

mediumfixed"breakdown" does not mean quietanalysis/src/arrange.ts:155

Quantile labelling guarantees roughly the bottom 30% of segments are called breakdown however flat the track is. Across the cached tracks, breakdown energy spans 29 to 89 and groove spans 64 to 94: 25 points of overlap. One shipped section calls itself a breakdown while ranking third loudest in its track. Fixed: re-measured on 374 SALAMI tracks the overlap was 28 points and 98% of tracks had a breakdown whether or not they had one. The quantile is replaced by a material step below the track's own median, the mirror of the DROP_STEP test that already gates the drop label. Overlap 28 to 12 points, tracks with a breakdown 98% to 84%, pairwise F 52.7 to 53.8.

3  Kick and bass

The detector is reasonable. What happens to its output afterwards is not.

criticalfixedThe kick curve peaks 24-42 ms behind the curve the grid is fitted toanalysis/src/drums.ts:31

Two different onset functions feed the two things that must agree. Measured signed offset to the nearest sixteenth: the beat-path curve sits at +2.9 to +5.3 ms, the kick curve at +24.1 to +42.1 ms. Every kick flash is systematically late, and nothing corrects it. Fixed: the cause is not the max filter this page suspected, because snare and hat run the same path and are punctual. It is that a 93 ms window cannot localise a 50 Hz event, so the low band is still climbing most of a window after the drum was struck. Which frames are kicks is still decided by the kick curve; WHERE they are is now taken from the broadband curve the grid itself was fitted to, bounded at half a sixteenth. Median offset 24.1 ms to 0.5 ms with the detection count unchanged; snare 4.5 to -0.3, hat 6.7 to 1.3.

criticalfixed19.6% of shipped kicks were invented, not detectedanalysis/src/quantise.ts:86

Pattern completion fills slots no detection ever occupied: 1249 of 6379 kicks across the corpus, ranging 5.6% to 27% per track. Nothing is ever dropped, only added. Meanwhile the guard meant to reject far-off onsets is unreachable, since rounding to the nearest slot bounds the error at exactly half a slot. Fixed: the shape is Yoshii et al. (ICASSP 2006): read the pattern bottom-up from the imperfect onset sequence, then treat every disagreement with it as an error CANDIDATE and verify each one against the audio before acting. That verification is the half that did not exist. The pattern is now pooled per repeat group rather than per fixed 8-bar window, weighted by each detection's own strength, and it may remove a hit as well as add one. Invented 19.6% to 4.5% of shipped kicks, with 19.1% of raw detections demoted.

Fixed: the gap is now a fraction of a sixteenth rather than of a beat. Roll recall 0.41 to 1.00 at 120 bpm and 0.004 to 1.00 at 175, both at precision 1.000. Kicks per track rise 335 to 452 and the sixteenth-slot histogram stays concentrated - 58% of kicks in the busiest four of sixteen slots against 25% for an even spread - so the extra hits are pattern rather than strobe.

highfixedThe bass-subtraction defence has no fixed meaninganalysis/src/drums.ts:111

The second half of this finding was wrong and the measurement says so: removing the defence entirely dropped fixture precision from 1.000 to 0.529, because a 110 Hz bass note leaks into the 20-90 Hz band far more than that band's 0.018 response at 110 Hz suggests.

On the fixture, whose ground truth is exact: snare recall 0.653 to 1.000 and hat F 0.923 to 0.952, with kick F holding at 1.000.

And pairwise F at 51.6% is almost exactly what the literature reports for labelling an entire track with a single label, which is the quantitative signature of the collapse this page opened with.

The fix holds. Replacing the length-normalised objective and separating identity from label moved boundary F3 up a quarter in relative terms, and moved the two structural figures much further: sections found went from 8.4 to 11.5 against 11.7 annotated, and the longest section halved, from 42.6% of the track to 21.1%. The third pass found two causes and both were structural. The first is the one the second pass named: similarityMatrix could not see level, so a drop and the groove behind it playing the same figure were literally identical rows. The second was not suspected at all. Every boundary was being dragged onto a global 4-bar phrase grid, and with a four-bar grid the worst move is two bars, which is four seconds at 120 bpm - outside the window the strict metric calls a hit, and outside the loose one as well. Snapping only what is within a bar of the grid was worth 1.7 points of F0.5 on its own, more than the level term bought.

That has a consequence worth stating plainly, because it changes a contract: music does move off a four-bar phrase, after an inserted break or a bar of 2/4, and a global anchor cannot follow it. The linter now accepts a cue that lands on a bar the analyser named as a section start, on the grounds that its rule is about not INVENTING an off-phrase change rather than about overruling a measurement. An invented off-phrase cue still errors.

Section count now overshoots - 16.3 against 11.7 annotated - and that is the honest cost of the trade. Under-segmentation fell with it and over-segmentation rose, which is the expected shape; the boundary F-measures are precision-and-recall balanced and both rose, so the extra boundaries are not free hits. 31.0% is still below the three-second baseline and well below the 0.6061 Marmoret et al. report on this corpus.

5  Effect rotation and colour

Reported from the room, and corroborated by the code: the show reaches for too few of its own effects, changes look at moments the music did not choose, and lights its biggest instant in colours the track never declared.

highfixedSix effects ignore the show palette entirelycore/src/effects/

auroraBorealis, chromaBurst, hueCarousel, iridescence, rainbowRain and vortex never read ctx.palette or SLOT; they call hsv2rgb with hues of their own. mediumfixedThe rotation reaches for a fraction of the catalogauthor-engine/src/select.ts:42

criticalfixedThe quiet sections are not dim, they are offcore/src/mixer.ts:139

Reported from the room twice, because the first answer was too small. Measured in the only unit that decides whether a passage is visible - the byte the LED receives - an intro averaged 2.9 of 255 with 5% of the room lit, an outro 4.4 with 18%, a breakdown 3.7 with 15%, against 134 and 88% for a drop. A ratio of 37 to 1, with the bottom of it below the threshold of seeing.

No amount of tuning reaches this, and that is the finding. A bed is written to sit UNDER something, so a cue carrying a bed and one texture emits about 0.2 before the output chain; gamma 2.2 turns 0.2 into byte 8 and 0.08 into byte 0, so even at an intensity of 1.0 such a cue tops out around byte 12. The dimmer was never the problem: the room was not being lit. Six effect gains, two palette-slot corrections, an extra layer per quiet cue and a thin-stack correction to intensity were all tried first, and between them they moved an intro from byte 3 to byte 13. Fixed with the thing every venue has and this room did not: a house floor. The mixer lifts the black point to a per-section level of the show's own base colour, after the cue's intensity rather than before it, because a breakdown asking for 40% is asking for 40% of its LOOK and not for the room to go out. A lift rather than a clamp, so the cue's layers still ride on top instead of being erased by it - clamping made the outro a dead flat wash whose peak equalled its floor. Auto-exposure may now only ever lift, since pulling a correctly lit room back toward its own target is undoing that decision somewhere the show cannot see. Measured on the same tracks before and after: intro 2.9 to 43.8, outro 4.4 to 41.2, breakdown 3.7 to 32.4, and the share of the room lit at all 5-18% to 100%. The drop is untouched at 129 because the highlight compressor absorbs the floor there, so the ratio lands at 3.2 to 1 with nothing below the threshold of seeing. A void still measures exactly 0.0 with 0% lit, and a blackout hit zeroes the floor with the intensity: darkness is still an instruction the show can give.

highfixedThe room ignores the colour the record already hasanalysis/src/artwork.ts

A palette was chosen from tempo, mode and how squashed the master is. Those are properties of the recording, and none of them is what the record looks like: somebody already chose a colour for the sleeve, and a yellow record lit in teal is arguing with its own artwork. Fixed: the cover is decoded through ffmpeg to 48 pixels and its dominant hue taken by a saturation-weighted circular histogram, then the WHOLE palette is rotated so its base lands there. Rotating rather than overwriting keeps the 140-180 degrees between base and accent that the palette library exists to guarantee. Across the cached tracks the hue is found with a 0.31-0.92 share and every base now matches its cover. One trap, and it is the reason for a test: a ceiling on value meant to drop blown highlights threw away the answer, because a flat saturated colour is full-value by construction - a sheet of yellow had every yellow pixel discarded and came back reading the cyan of the character drawn on it.

highfixedThe tempo correction cannot reach the readings that need itanalysis/src/metricalLevel.ts

The control shipped, and then did not offer the right answer. Two faults, one cause. Halving was gated on alternate beats being weak, which is structurally false for the case that matters: a double-time reading of a rock track has real hats on the beats a halved reading would drop, so Hallowed Be Thy Name at 200 bpm offered nothing at all. And two-against-three was offered only upward, so Obchodník s deštěm - tracked at 132 in 6/4 where it is played at 88 in 4/4 - could be doubled or halved but never corrected. Fixed: the relatives are 1/2, 2/3, 3/2 and 2, offered on the tempo prior alone and gated on no local discriminator, because this page already recorded that the obvious one came out anti-correlated with the truth. All four ear-judged disputes are now reachable, including Melanž at 118 against a true 120, which is the 2:3 case a half/double control could never have repaired. ambiguous now means "this is an unusual place for a tactus" and decides only how prominently to offer the correction; the alternatives are there on every track, because the only reliable judge of metrical level is the person in the room.

Drum detection is reported as sometimes plainly wrong, and the fix worth researching is repetition. Percussion here is overwhelmingly periodic, and that redundancy is evidence the detector does not use: the present pattern completion votes over fixed 8-bar windows, ignores how confident each detection was, and can only add hits, never remove or move one. That is why a fifth of shipped kicks are fabricated. SectionSpan.group now gives repeat identity for free, so two sections of the same group should carry the same drum pattern and any disagreement is either a fill or a detector error.

Why a hand-rolled fix cannot close this. Holding the current beats and meter and choosing only the correct bar phase would lift downbeat F from 0.498 to 0.740; a perfect meter on top adds just 0.023. So phase is 97% of the local headroom, and downbeat F tracks phase accuracy almost exactly: 0.740 × 0.675 = 0.4995 against a measured 0.498.

The current picker gets the phase right 67.5% of the time, and the harmonic-change term alone also scores 67.5%, so the other four terms of the weighted sum contribute nothing. I tried the obvious repairs: template matching reached 71.4%, a trained softmax over bar rotations 72.7%. Published work puts hand-crafted features through the best available aggregator at 0.645. A bar-pointer DBN would not help either, because with a fixed meter its Viterbi is mathematically identical to the softmax already measured.

Where this leaves the routes

The metrical level splits 2-2, judged by ear

On the fifteen cached tracks the two systems agree on metrical level for eight and disagree for seven, six of those by a clean factor. No objective measure available here can adjudicate: any "how close is the nearest beat" proxy prefers a faster grid mechanically, because a half-time grid has twice the gap. So the disputes were settled by listening to an A/B click test.

Both systems do get the grid right up to a metrical factor about 95% of the time (Acc2 97.0% and 94.9%), so the error is nearly always a clean doubling or halving, and that is what makes it tractable separately.

Route A

Pure TypeScript

Krebs bar patterns, Harte's HCDF, kick and snare posteriors as downbeat features, per-section phrase anchors. Lands around downbeat F 0.60-0.64 with beat F unchanged, and keeps the zero-dependency property. It cannot reach the table above.

Route B, revised

The model supplies the grid; the metrical level stays local

Take Beat This!'s beat positions and downbeats, where it measurably wins, and do not take its tempo octave. Decide the level in a dedicated stage: a half-time reading is a subset of the true beats, so inserting midpoints and scoring them against the onset curve tests it directly, and a double-time reading is a superset that thins the same way. That is what barCoherence already attempts, now operating on a much better grid.

Then surface disagreement rather than hiding it. The two systems agreed on eight of fifteen tracks; where they do not, that is a well-calibrated flag for a half/double correction in the app, which a listener resolves in seconds and the cache keeps.

Built. TempoGrid now ships ambiguous and alternativeBpm, the inspector offers the alternatives as buttons on exactly the tracks flagged, and analyzeTrack takes a metricalLevel multiplier. The re-read is an exact operation on the beat array rather than a re-detection, because a half-time reading is a SUBSET of the true beats and a double-time reading a superset; resampling in beat-index space also covers the two-against-three case, which is what Melanž needed and which no half/double control could have repaired. The whole show is re-composed rather than rescaled, because every cue is addressed by bar and the bars have moved.

What is measured and what is not

  • Measured: tempo, beats, downbeats and meter against 764 annotated tracks; kick invention and displacement against the shipped cache; contrast, limiting and hit coverage by running the real player and mixer.
  • Newly possible: sections, against Harmonix (222 tracks with audio, the right repertoire, offset-corrected) and SALAMI (aligned by construction). Boundary F0.5/F3, pairwise and over/under-segmentation are wired and validated; the baseline run is queued.
  • Settled by ear, because nothing else could: metrical level on the disputed tracks. bench/clicktest.ts pans the two candidate grids hard apart at different pitches, so where they agree the clicks fuse and where they disagree they separate.
  • Still by eye: whether the light feels right. bench/render.ts renders any show to video with its own audio muxed, from the same mixer bytes the LEDs receive, which is how the 4.85 s drift was confirmed as visible rather than merely tabulated. bench/flashprobe.ts is the readable version: the kick band's envelope and the show's kickEnv printed side by side, frame by frame, so a flash that lands late shows up as a lagging column rather than as a feeling.
  • Newly measured in the third pass: colour identity, by taking the hue of every lit byte the LEDs receive and asking whether the palette the effect was handed that frame could have produced it; delivered contrast, as the drop-to-quiet ratio of those same bytes undone through gamma; catalog reach and peak variety over 380 seeded shows; and which planned hits ever actually reach the room. bench/flashprobe.ts is the readable form of the render check: the kick band's envelope and the show's kickEnv printed side by side, frame by frame, so a flash that lands late shows as a lagging column rather than as a feeling.
  • Newly measured in the fourth pass: what each section label means in DRUMS rather than in energy, per beat and per track, which is what said the seven-value vocabulary was recoverable from the kit before any of it was built (bench/labelprobe.ts); how much the room MOVES and how much SHAPE it has, as bytes of frame-to-frame change and of within-frame spread, which is the only way "plain colour with a little drift" is a number rather than an opinion (bench/levelprobe.ts); and where punctuation starts, how long it holds the room in bars and in seconds, and whether the linter still rejects the things it is supposed to (bench/punctprobe.ts, which runs the real linter rather than reimplementing its rules, so a rule that quietly stops being enforced shows up as a number instead of as silence).
  • Iteration cost, which decided how much of this was possible: a full SALAMI pass is 7 minutes 36 seconds, and almost all of it is decode, spectrogram and beat tracking, none of which any change to the segmenter can touch. Freezing everything upstream of the bar table to disk turned that into 4.4 seconds, reproducing the full run's figures exactly, which is the difference between sweeping four constants and sweeping forty.
